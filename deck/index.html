Your transaction runs twice; the first time is a rehearsal. Forklift lifts the Portaldot V3 testnet at one block, 118,061, onto your machine, checks the fork against the live chain, 200 of 200 sampled keys identical, then runs your signed bytes on the fork before the same bytes go to V3. Three real V3 transactions were sent this way, each with the same tx hash as predicted.

Bench · your machine

portaldot_dev --dev

  • One validator, named Alice.
  • None of the real chain's accounts, stakes or nominators.
  • Writable, and free to break, but not the state your users are on.

A builder who wants to test one staking call, or one contract, against the state their users are actually on has no simple way to get that state onto a local node, and no way to check that what they got is faithful.

Two places to try a transaction, and neither is right. The local dev chain is writable but empty: one validator called Alice. The V3 testnet has real state, but every transaction is final and the fee moves every block. Forklift is the forklift between them.

Architecture · forklift/*.py at build 44b7edc

Rack → forklift → bench

Left is the rack: the live chains, read at a pinned block hash. The middle is Forklift's Python modules. Right is the bench: local forks on loopback. The V3 fork runs on Chopsticks with V3's own runtime; the mainnet fork runs on the official portaldot_dev node. The one write to a real chain is rehearse --send, and only to V3.
Why it's new

Predict. Send. Compare.

One signature, both sides

The extrinsic is signed once, so the tx hash on the fork is the tx hash on V3. The prediction and the chain's record are about the same bytes.

Wrong is an exit code

When the chain differs, rehearse exits 7 and lists each field. A predicted failure exits 5 and sends nothing (an unfunded signer: the fork's pool rejected it).

Fidelity you can re-run

forklift replay <block>-<index> re-applies any V3 extrinsic on a fork of its parent block and compares it with the chain.

Pinned by what the chain serves

Genesis plus runtime :code hash, not a URL. Before predicting, the fork is advanced toward the chain's best block, and the fee multiplier is re-synced if it differs.

What is new is the loop: predict on a fork of V3's real state, send the same bytes, and compare. When the chain disagrees, the tool says so with an exit code instead of adjusting the prediction. And replay lets anyone check the fork's fidelity on any V3 extrinsic.

Business potential

Who lifts, and how it pays.

Builders

Test one staking call or one contract against the state users are actually on, before spending tPOT on a transaction that is final.

Node operators

Rehearse an operational transaction on a fork of the chain they run, and see the events, fee and balance deltas before sending.

Wallets & exchanges

Predict the outcome of a transaction on a pinned copy of the chain inside CI, and fail the job when the chain would differ.
